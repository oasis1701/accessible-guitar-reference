Accessible Guitar Reference

Chord charts, fretboard diagrams, and video lessons all assume you can see them. This site is a set of guitar guides and tools for blind and visually impaired players, and it describes everything in words. Each description says which string, which fret, and which finger, phrased the same way every time.

Start here

Read How to read this site first. It explains the grammar every description follows, including how strings are numbered, what higher and lower mean, how muted strings are announced, and how movable shapes work.

Guides and tools

  • Fretboard guide

    Find the note at every fret of every string, from the open string to the 12th fret, with the landmark frets marked. Four octave shapes then let two strings you know well lead you to every note on the other four.

  • Open chords

    Learn the fourteen everyday chords played near the nut, often called cowboy chords. There are five major chords, three minor chords, and six dominant 7th chords.

  • Power chords

    Power chords use just the root and the fifth, and are written with a 5, as in E5. Learn the open E5, A5, and D5, and movable two-finger and three-finger shapes for any root note.

  • Barre chords

    Turn the open E and A grips into any major or minor chord along the neck, with four movable barre shapes.

  • Triads

    Play compact three-note major and minor shapes in all three inversions, on strings 1 to 3, 2 to 4, 3 to 5, and 4 to 6.

  • Chord finder

    Choose any root note, major or minor, to see every way this site knows to play that chord, ordered along the neck.

  • Tuner

    Tune with your microphone. The tuner names the closest note and says how many cents too low or too high it is, and its spoken updates can be muted.

  • Metronome

    Practice with a click at any tempo from 30 to 500 beats per minute. It can accent the first beat, add subdivisions, take its tempo from your tapping, and raise the tempo gradually as you play.

Make it read your way

On the Settings page you can choose how descriptions are written, either per string from thickest to thinnest, by finger, or as compact prose. You can also choose whether strings are named by number, by note name, or both. Your choice applies to every description on the site.

NVDA add-on for Neural DSP plug-ins

The author of this site also makes Neural DSP Plugins Accessibility, an add-on for the NVDA screen reader that makes the Archetype Petrucci X, Archetype Nolly X, and Fortin Nameless Suite X guitar plug-ins usable with a screen reader. Its page links to the latest release and the keyboard guide.

Feedback

The site's source code and issue tracker are at Accessible Guitar Reference on GitHub. Corrections and suggestions are welcome, especially from other blind players. Open an issue there and describe what reads poorly or what is missing.
